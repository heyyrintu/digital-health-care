# Access Control Policy

**Status:** Draft · **Owner:** Tech lead

## Principles
Least privilege; access by role and organisation; clinical data only for treating doctors and the patient; every record view of clinical data is logged.

## Roles
Patient, Doctor, Front desk, Clinic Admin, Platform Admin (no clinical access), Verifier (public QR page), Visitor (public doctor pages). Permission matrix: PRD v3.0 §3.2.

## Public doctor pages
A doctor publishes their own page (`/doctor/<slug>`) from the profile page: the address, whether it is listed, an about text and languages (`PATCH /doctor-profile/public`, doctor role, own profile only; audited as `doctor_profile.public_updated` with field names only). Clinic admins and the front desk cannot edit it, as for the prescription pad. Anyone may read a page without signing in, but only while it is listed and the platform team has verified the registration, the doctor is an active doctor of an active organisation, and only the published fields: name, qualifications, specialty, verified registration, about, languages, the organisation's name and slug, the clinics (name, address) and consultation types (name, mode, minutes, fee) the doctor is scheduled for. The home page (`/`) lists every page shown as a card (name, qualifications, specialty, the organisation's name, the scheduled clinics' names and the first line of their addresses, languages and the lowest fee), so a visitor can find doctors without knowing their address; it shows nothing a doctor's page does not. The read runs as `dhc_app` with no organisation set and goes only through the `public_doctor_*` database functions (`public_doctor_profile`, `public_doctor_slugs`, `public_doctor_directory`; ADR 0017), so it cannot reach patients or any other tenant row.

## Organisation branding
Only clinic admins read and change their organisation's branding: the display name, accent colour, tagline and logo (`GET`/`PUT /organisation-branding`, `POST`/`DELETE /organisation-branding/logo`; doctors, the front desk and patients get 403, anonymous requests 401). Every change is audited as `organisation.branding_updated` with the changed field names only. The admin endpoints run inside the admin's organisation (`withTenant`): `organisation_branding` has row-level security, and a CHECK keeps a logo's file key inside the organisation's own folder. Anyone may read the branding of an **active** organisation that saved it, without signing in: `GET /public/organisations/{slug}/branding` (its slug and name, already public through its doctors' pages and clinic links, and the four branding fields) and the logo itself at `GET /public/logos/{sha256}`; the doctor pages carry the display name, accent and logo address, and the home page's cards the display name only. These reads go only through `public_organisation_branding`, `public_organisation_logo` and `public_doctor_directory` (owner, `SECURITY DEFINER`, executable only by `dhc_app` with no organisation set; ADR 0017 addendum 2). A suspended organisation's branding and logo stop answering at the API at once, and everywhere within five minutes (the caches); a logo is served from another active organisation's copy of the same bytes, never a suspended one's. The patient portal reads the branding without the patient's token.

## Joiners and leavers
Accounts created by role on joining; removed the same day on leaving (staff devices revoked, cloud and store access removed, platform console operator disabled, keys rotated if they had access).

## Clinic staff
Clinic Admin manages staff accounts and devices; front desk sees no clinical content; chart model (shared vs own patients) set per organisation. The clinic admin's medicine list and approval queue show medicine names typed by doctors with usage counts only, never patients or prescriptions. Patient merges are asked for by front desk, doctors and clinic admins and decided only by a clinic admin; the comparison shows demographics and visit counts, never clinical content.

Test orders (PRD §7.1): only the visit's doctor writes them, with the prescription draft, and they lock when it is signed. Doctors read a patient's orders and open result files under the chart model (a doctor's own orders are always open to them); only doctors mark results reviewed. The front desk lists a patient's ordered tests without the doctor's notes and uploads results that come to the counter, but cannot open result files. Clinic admins have no access to orders or results; they keep the test catalogue (add, rename, edit regions and views, reorder, deactivate; never delete), which holds test definitions (kind, name, body regions, X-ray views), never patients or orders, and their edits never change a test already ordered. Doctors read only the active catalogue. Catalogue changes are audited. Patients see and upload against the ordered tests of their own account's patients and dependants, and open those results. Listing (by staff and in the Patient app), uploading, opening and reviewing are audited; the doctor's results-to-review worklist is not, like the queue. The public QR check shows the test names a prescription printed, never notes or results.

Referrals (PRD §7.2): doctors only. The visit's doctor makes them, once the patient has checked in; only the referring and the receiving doctor read a referral (anyone else, and another organisation, gets 404), only the receiving doctor's opening marks it seen, and either of them closes it. The front desk and clinic admins get 403 on every referral route: a referral's reason and notes are clinical content, and those roles have no access to history, notes or diagnoses (PRD §3.2); the admin's audit log shows referral events by ID only. **Chart model:** under "own patients only" a doctor reads a patient's chart, visit notes, prescriptions and test orders once the patient has been checked in to them, or while an internal referral of the patient to them is open (sent or seen) and the visit it was made at still stands (checked in, in consultation or completed). Closing the referral, or cancelling that visit, ends that access; an external referral grants nothing. It is the same access as having seen the patient (the chart's entries can be kept up to date); the referring visit, its notes and its prescription stay the referring doctor's to write. Audited as `referral.created`, `referral.viewed`, `referral.seen` and `referral.closed`, `referral.visit_viewed` for a visit's referrals and `referral.listed` for each Received or Sent list (which names patients and outside recipients; the entry holds the box and count only), with IDs, kind and urgency only, never the patient, the reason, the notes or the outside recipient.
